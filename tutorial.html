Starting Out

Kerbal Space Program is a very fun, but also complicated game. Because of this, there is a steep learning curve when you first start playing. This guide is here to help you learn some of the basics, however, there is also an in-game interactive tutorial that you can follow!


Your First Game

In KSP, you have three main gamemodes that you can chose to play:

  • Sandbox Mode: Allows you to easily experiment without having to deal with funding, nor science points
  • Science Mode: Slightly more challenging, as you start with limited access to rocket parts, and have to unlock new technology by conducting "science" experiments
  • Career Mode: The ultimate challenge! You have to not only deal with the challenges of space flight, but also tech-tree progression, funding, contracts, and your space agency's reputation

When you start your first game in Kerbal Space Program, you will see a overhead view of your digital space center. You can click on the different buildings to open menus assosiated with them! A common starting place is the V.A.B, where you can start designing your first rocket. The rocket editor allows you to select a "root" part, and then attach other parts to that "root" part. Once you have built your first rocket, you can press the green launch button in the upper-right corner of the screen to send your rocket to the launchpad!


Basic Flight Controls

You can use Z to fully throttle up, and X to fully throttle down, as well as Shift and Ctrl for more precise adustments to your throttle. You can press Space to activate the next stage of your rocket (see the staging bar on the left side of your flight HUD). Once you have launched your rocket, you can use the W A S D keys to control the attitude of your rocket. If you find your rocket flipping over, you can always enable SAS by pressing T. Finally, you can also press M to open a map/trajectory view.



Space and Beyond

Once you have gotten to space, you are going to need to know how to get into orbit, change your orbit, and maybe even visit the moon.


Orbits

Contary to popular beleif, when you are orbiting a planet, you are still affected by gravity, and you are still technically falling. You are also just moving side-ways so fast that you never hit the ground; you just continue to fall around the planet!

You can acheive orbit in KSP by angling your rocket somewhat sideways while flying. A 45 degree angle is a good place to start.

Source: Image Taken by Creator of Website, of video game Kerbal Space Program, created by Squad

Once you have acheived a stable orbit, you can burn your engine in certain directions relative to the body that you are orbiting around, to change your orbit. While orbital mechanics are complicated, there are a few key things you should know. To start off, go to the map screen, enable SAS (press T), and look at the navigation ball at the bottom-center of your screen. There should be a few butons beside it, which are attitude presets. The first tries to keep your rocket's attitude from changing from it's current attitude, and the second and third are Prograde and Retrograde respectively. If you burn your engine while facing Prograde, the other side of your orbit will extend. If you burn retrograde, the other side of your orbit will start lowering.

Kerbin's Moons

In KSP, Kerbin has two moons that you can orbit and land (or crash) on:

  • The Mün: The equivalent of Earth's moon
  • Minmus: A smaller moon, further out from Kerbin, in a slightly eliptical orbit.

Once you have acheived orbit around Kerbin, it is fairly easy to get to the Mün and or Minmus. Just use a prograde burn to extend one side of your orbit until your trajectory line roughly crosses where the moon that you are aiming for will be in a few days! If your burn is succesful, the map screen should show a circular icon where your trajectory will intercept the Sphere of Influence of that moon. Use time warping (upper-left corner) to speed up time until you reach the periapsis of your trajectory around the moon, then burn retrograde to slow down and capture into an orbit around the moon!

From there, you can burn retrograde to enter into a sub-orbital trajectory relative to the moon. Just remember to fire your engines retrograde every now and then on your descent to slow yourself down! (Or else)



Interplanetary Missions

Have you conquered both of Kerbin's moons? Looking for an even harder challenge? With the help of this article, you can learn to go to other planets in the Kerbol System! Be warned. This is very difficult.


Transfer Windows

Timing is one of the most important parts of an interplanetary mission in KSP. You have to leave the Sphere of Influence of Kerbin at a certain time, and in a certain direction, so as not to waste massive amounts of fuel. First, get into a circular orbit around Kerbin at an altitude of around 100km from the surface. Go to the map screen, and zoom out until you can see the entire Kerbol system. Decide on which planet you want to go to (I recommend Duna for beginners), and look up your destination in an online planetary phase angle chart or calculator like the one made by Olexandr Savchuk, which allows you to enter in a starting planet and target planet (https://ksp.olex.biz/).

These charts and or calculators should show you what the angle between Kerbin, Kerbol, and your target planet should be when you start your transfer. Use KSP's timewarp feature to fast forward time until the angle shown in the chart matches what you see on the KSP map screen. Next, check if the average distance of your target planet from Kerbol (the sun) is higer or lower than Kerbin's.

  • If it is higher than Kerbin's, raise one side of your orbit around Kerbin so that it sets you on an escape trajectory from Kerbin that is prograde to Kerbin's orbit around the sun (this will raise your orbit around the sun a bit higher, helping to match that your target planet).
  • If it is lower than Kerbin's, raise one side of your orbit around Kerbin so that it sets you on an escape trajectory from Kerbin that is retrograde to Kerbin's orbit around the sun (this will lower your orbit around the sun, helping to match your target planet's orbit).

Once you have escaped Kerbin's Sphere of Influence, you will have to set a maneuver node (a planned engine burn) to acheive and fine-tune an intercept with your target planet. First, click on your target planet, and click "Set as Target". Next, click on part of your trajectory line (on the map screen) slightly after where your ship currently is, and click "Add Maneuver Node". A special three-dimensonal editor should show up with sliders for multiple directions including Prograde, and Retrograde. Moving these sliders will change the direction and length of the burn that you are planning. You will have to tweak these sliders around a lot until you get an actual intercept with your target planet, but just remember that Prograde will raise the other side of your orbit, and Retrograde will lower it.

Once your maneuver node's planned trajectory shows an intercept with your target planet, you will have to wait until (or time-warp to) your maneuver node (Hint: There should be a few extra maneuver-node/burn related indicators next to your navigation-ball). Your navigation ball should now show an extra blue maneuver marker. Use its SAS preset, or manual attitude adjustments, to point your ship towards the marker. Once you are facing in the right direction, you are ready to start your transfer burn! Try timing it so that your ship "passes through" the maneuver node about half-way through your burn.

Remember, this takes lots of practice, and you might not get it right on your first attempt. Keep trying! Additionally, if you get a trajectory close to what you needed, but no intercept indicator on your real trajectory, try doing a few extra correction burns a bit closer to your target planet. Remember to make save points in your game so that you can revert to a previous point in your game if something goes wrong!


Final Tips

Once you have entered into another planet's Sphere of Influence, you can burn your engine retrograde at your periapsis around the planet to slow down and capture into orbit. From there, if you are planning on attempting a landing, the process varies slightly from planet to planet. When designing your lander, you should keep in mind the following:

  • Does the planet have an atmosphere? If it does:
    1. Is it a thick atmosphere? --> Parachutes
    2. Will you enter the atmosphere at a fast speed? --> Heatshields
  • Are you planning on returning to space? --> Ascent Stage
  • Do you have an antenna on your lander for communications? Can the signal be somehow relayed back to Kerbin? --> Send a Relay Satalite
  • Do you have a wide base? Will your center of mass be close to the ground? (Prevents flipping)
  • Do you have redundant systems in case something goes wrong? (For example, what if you forgot to transfer a pilot to your lander? Do you have a basic probe-core that will allow you to still have basic controls over your ship?)
